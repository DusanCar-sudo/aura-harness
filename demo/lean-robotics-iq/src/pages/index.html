01 The problem The English-only default

The instruction layer defaults to English. The deployment doesn't.

Robot policies are conditioned on language. That language is written once, in English, by whoever collected the data — and then inherited by every model, lab and factory downstream.

A vision-language-action model learns from triples: an observation, an instruction, an action. The observation is a camera. The action is a robot. The instruction is a sentence — and in the public corpus, that sentence is almost always English.

Open X-Embodiment pools 60 robot datasets and more than a million trajectories; DROID contributes 76,000 trajectories across 564 scenes; RT-1 carries 130,000 episodes; BridgeData V2 adds 60,000 more. The documented annotation examples are English — while the index that pools them for the field records annotation style (templated, natural, none), and has no locale field at all. In the current index, about a third of the pooled datasets carry no language annotations yet.

When a policy lands in a kitchen in Da Nang or a warehouse in Novi Sad, there are only three options: fine-tune the language away, keep a translation layer between operator and machine, or accept that the robot only understands the people who collected its data. None of those is free.

“An instruction is not a sentence. It is a specification of an action. Translate the words and miss the referent, and you have changed the task.”

MODE 01

Referent drift

The blue block is masculine in one language and feminine in another. Pronouns, agreement and noun-class systems have to resolve to the same object in the scene — not merely the same dictionary entry.

MODE 02

Alignment loss

A translated string must stay keyed to the same timestep, subtask and action segment. A paraphrase that shifts a boundary corrupts the label even when the sentence reads perfectly.

MODE 03

Affordance mismatch

“Put it in the microwave” carries a kitchen layout, a button and a height. Localized data has to describe the affordance the robot will actually meet, not the one in the original scene.

Prior art, acknowledged

Translation for robot learning is not a blank field. LIBERO has been translated into ten languages for evaluation, and VLA success dropped 30–50% under non-English instructions (ACL 2026). A 2026 Greek VLA study found machine translation alone recovered about two fifths of English performance — and that policies overfit the translator's phrasing; seven phrasings per task roughly halved that penalty. Community datasets translate individual tasks. What we could not find is dataset-side localization in the native format, with per-episode provenance and an independent verifier report. That is the line we build — and we date-stamp the absence claim instead of calling it a first. Read the sources →

03 The localization line Sample, not a dataset

The sentence survives the move. The action spine is preserved.

One manipulation instruction, five localizations, and the decision a competent translator has to make — the part a dictionary cannot see.

A single instruction from a manipulation-style dataset: pick up the blue block, place it in the red bowl.

Each localization carries the same action spine — reach → grasp → lift → move → place — keyed to the same segment boundaries. The tabs are not a translation memory. They show the failure modes we are paid to catch.

Grammar“Kocka” (block) is feminine, so the adjective is plavu and the clitic is je. A noun-agnostic template produces broken agreement and an ambiguous referent. SpineSegment boundaries unchanged; the two verb clauses map one-to-one. ReviewNative reviewer, two passes; one back-translation spot check per 50 strings.
EN → DE · GERMANOPENING LINE

source string“Pick up the blue block and place it in the red bowl.”

Nimm den blauen Klotz und lege ihn in die rote Schale.

Take the blue block and lay it into the red bowl.

GrammarTwo genders in one sentence: den blauen Klotz (accusative masculine) and die rote Schale (accusative feminine). The pronoun ihn must agree with the block, not the bowl. RegisterDu-imperative matches the lab convention of the source data; switching to “Sie” changes the perceived distance between operator and robot.
EN → VI · VIETNAMESEPILOT LINE

source string“Pick up the blue block and place it in the red bowl.”

SpecificityXanh dương (blue) versus xanh lá (green) is the most common failure when English “blue/green” is translated without the scene. The classifier khối carries the object class. SpineVerb chain preserved; the pronoun nó is used once, where Vietnamese would not repeat it.
EN → ES · SPANISHOPENING LINE

GrammarColócalo: the enclitic lo agrees with bloque. Splitting the pronoun changes the referent in fast operator speech. RegionCoge (Spain) versus toma / agarra (parts of Latin America). The line is region-tagged, not just language-tagged.

04 The method Recursive learning × the Archimedes principle

A loop that remembers. A gate that refuses to guess.

Two mechanisms from the founder's preprint, applied to data work — an adaptation we state plainly on the method page. The loop turns every localized episode into memory; the gate decides when a cheap tier has displaced enough verified work to be trusted with the next line.

Recursive learning is an operator over an episode store: execute, capture, consolidate, inject. The Archimedes Principle is a competence gate: a cheap tier displaces expensive work only in proportion to verified acceptance.

Together they make a cost–quality claim precise enough to be wrong. The gate pays only when the verified acceptance rate q clears the tier cost ratio, and its quality is exactly as good as its verifier — a bound, not a promise. We use the same decision layer on translation: a fast first pass may carry a line only where a measured acceptance rate clears the ratio; a human reviewer is the large tier; and the verifier is never the producer.

05 Evidence The deployment audit

We reported the failure before the product.

Most vendors show a benchmark. Here is a deployment audit in full, including the part where the mechanism never fired.

Across 153 instrumented alternation decisions, the cheap tier was attempted zero times. Every one of the 143 cost-ledger rows reads direct-to-large. The gate was not wrong. It was never reached.

The cause was not a competence judgement. A local backend was unreachable, and the system recorded an unreachable backend as a non-attempt — which is logically indistinguishable from “no evidence yet.” The estimator froze in its permissive branch and no amount of data could have moved it. The paper predicted this non-identifiability; the deployment exhibited it.

The second cause was structural. All 143 pattern keys were unique, so no task ever accumulated the three prior neighbours the gate needs before it can evaluate a success rate. The maximum neighbourhood size ever observed was two. A gate whose patterns do not repeat is a gate that cannot open.

This is on a company site because our product is verified translation. A verification company that hides its own null result is selling confidence, not evidence. The preprint also states what the audit cannot establish — that gating saves money, that the cheap tier clears the threshold, that the verifier is sound — and closes with a pre-registered four-arm protocol that would settle it.

Six findings, reported without selection

  1. The gate was never exercised. The small tier was invoked zero times across 153 instrumented tasks.
  2. It was structurally unreachable. No task accumulated the three required prior neighbours; the maximum was two, the median zero.
  3. The pattern vocabulary does not repeat. All 143 pattern keys and all 143 eight-token slugs were unique: K ≈ n.
  4. The fallback is adequate but unexamined. 93.5% of escalated answers were approved — and with no small-tier answers, that says nothing about what the gate would have bought.
  5. The price of the null regime is the full price. 7.07M tokens at 49.5k per task, 8.6 s median latency: identical to a system with no gate.
  6. Non-identifiability appeared exactly as predicted. An absent backend produced episodes indistinguishable from absent evidence.
