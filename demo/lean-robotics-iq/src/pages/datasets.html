Deliverables · protocol v0.4

The work · what leaves the building

A dataset goes in. A record comes out.

A localization delivery is not a translated file. It is the localized data plus the evidence that every line still means the action it names: a verifier report, a provenance ledger, a termbase, and the questions the data could not settle.

01 The anatomy Two bundles, one packet

Input is a promise. Output is evidence.

A delivery is two bundles that have to match: what you hand over, and what comes back with a record attached. If the second cannot be checked against the first, the first was not a delivery.

Input bundle — what we need
  • The export, plus one readable sample.The dataset in its native format, and a sample shard we are allowed to read end to end.
  • The schema and the field map.Where instructions, subtasks, captions and success criteria live; which fields are source and which are derived.
  • Segment alignment.Where each string starts and ends against the action timeline, with timestep or frame ids.
  • Target languages and variants.Script preferences (Latin or Cyrillic), region tags and register conventions, decided once.
  • Termbase, or permission to build one.Existing object names, verb conventions, units, safety phrasing and brand terms.
  • An evaluation slice.200–500 lines your own reviewers can score blind against the source scene.
  • A reviewer who can settle a referent.One person on your side who can answer “which block?” in under a day.
  • The legal basis.A licence for the source data, and confirmation that the ledger may be retained after delivery.
Output bundle — what ships
  • Localized data, keyed to source ids.New language fields beside the source. The source string is never overwritten.
  • The format adapter.A field map plus a validator that writes localized text into your schema and fails loudly when it does not fit.
  • The verifier report.Every line with its verdict, the check that failed, and the evidence the verifier used.
  • The provenance ledger.Line id, source, target, decision, verifier, reviewer, termbase version, timestamp.
  • The termbase artifact.Object names, verbs, units and register decisions as a versioned file — not a glossary in an email.
  • The adjudication log.Disputes and how they were resolved, kept as decisions rather than averaged into a score.
  • Open questions.Everything the data could not settle, with the options and our recommendation.
  • A re-run manifest.Pipeline version and settings, so a delta batch is comparable with the first delivery.

We do not need your model weights, and we do not train on your data. The input bundle is a reading and writing job, not a systems integration. If you want the argument for why this layer matters before the plumbing, it is on the front page: the instruction layer is monolingual, and the deployment is not.

02 Artifact families The typed units

Seven artifact types. One rule: the referent survives.

An instruction is not the only string that can break a policy. Captions, success criteria and failure notes carry the same referents — and the same ways of losing them.

Artifact Source example (EN) What is preserved Verifier check
instruction “Pick up the blue block and place it in the red bowl.” The action spine — reach, grasp, lift, move, place — with both referents bound to the same object instances in the scene. Referent identity; spine order; no constraint added or dropped.
subtask “grasp the blue block” The timestep range it labels and the verb class it asserts: grasp, not lift or move. Segment alignment against the action timeline.
caption “A robot arm lifts a blue block above a red bowl.” Object classes, spatial relations (above, inside, beside) and the recorded viewpoint. Frame of reference; nothing in the scene invented.
success criterion “The blue block rests inside the red bowl and the gripper is open.” The binary condition, object identity, spatial predicate and gripper state. Decidability: the condition can still be scored yes or no.
failure note “Block knocked the bowl off the table.” Event roles — actor, object, consequence — and the failure mode, without blame or interpretation. Event roles; tense and modality; no cause invented.
dialogue turn Operator: “Not that one — the blue block on the left.” Speaker, turn order, correction target and the deixis that points at it. Deixis; register; turn boundary.
eval rubric “3 — places the block in the bowl without knocking it over. 1 — drops it outside.” Score anchors, their order, and the criteria each anchor names. Numerals; scale direction; comparability with the source rubric.

Examples are illustrative strings written to a standard manipulation schema — not client data. Field names differ per dataset; the artifact type is what stays constant.

03 Format support Adapters, not conversions

The format stays yours. The language layer moves.

We do not convert your dataset into our format and hand it back. We read the format it already has, localize the language fields, and map the result to your schema with an adapter you keep.

01 / RLDS

Episodes, steps, sidecar

RLDS is the substrate under OXE, DROID and BridgeData V2: episodes of steps, with the instruction typically repeated on each step. Localized copies are keyed to the same episode and step ids; the source string stays in a sidecar.

02 / OPEN X-EMBODIMENT

One schema, sixty cards

The pool standardises episodes into a shared schema, yet each dataset card keeps its own vocabulary and annotation style. Each source gets its own field map; datasets whose card lists no annotations need an annotation pass first, scoped separately.

Tasks, IDs, locale files

04 / HDF5 · JSONL

Attributes and manifests

In LIBERO-style HDF5 the instruction sits in problem_info attributes on the demo group; in JSONL it is a per-line record. Provenance joins by path and pointer, not row number, so a re-export does not break the ledger.

Round-trip check

Nothing leaves the pipeline except the language fields — and the diff is the check. We localize, remove the localized fields, and compare the remainder against the source. If a non-language field moves, the delivery fails. Where a schema has no room for a parallel field, we say so at intake instead of overwriting the source.

Formats are mapped to the client's schema, not the other way round. The adapter is an artifact you keep: a field map and a validator, versioned with the delivery, that runs when the data grows rather than describing what happened once. If the validator cannot prove the round trip on your format, the honest answer is that the format needs an amendment — and we would rather say that before the batch than after it. The format and dataset references we check against are listed in the method page's references.

04 The verifier Seven checks + the hold rule

The verifier is not the producer.

Seven checks run as a separate pass, by a reviewer who does not see the producer's reasoning until the pass is closed. A line that fails any check does not ship quietly.

Every line is checked against the source scene, not only the source sentence. A translation can be grammatical and still change the task; the checks exist to catch that case, in this order.

Quality here is a bound, not a promise. The preprint's safety envelope (Proposition 6.11) says a gated system can never be safer than its verifier — adapted to this work, a delivery can never be safer than its review. So the delivery is built to let you inspect the verifier instead of trusting the producer. The report names each rejected line, the check that failed and the reviewer who settled it. The producer's confidence is not admissible evidence.

A line that fails any check is held, escalated to a named human reviewer, and either revised with the decision logged or delivered as an open question. It is never averaged into a score.

01 / REFERENT

Referent identity

The target phrase must denote the same object instance in the scene, not the same dictionary entry. “The blue block” is one block, not any blue block.

02 / SEGMENT

Segment alignment

Each string stays keyed to the same timestep, subtask and action segment. A paraphrase that shifts a boundary corrupts the label even when it reads well.

03 / GRAMMAR

Morphology and agreement

Gender, noun class, case and clitics. The pronoun has to resolve to the planned referent, not merely to a grammatical antecedent.

04 / QUANTITY

Numerals and units

Counts, fractions, dimensions, cm or mm, temperature, velocity. Conversion happens only as a logged decision; “two” is not “a couple”.

05 / FRAME

Deixis and frame of reference

Left, right, toward, behind — in the robot frame, the camera frame or the operator frame, whichever the dataset records. The frame is a field, not an assumption.

06 / REGISTER

Register

Formal or informal imperative, per dataset convention. The polite form is a product decision written down once, not a translator's mood.

07 / AFFORDANCE

Cultural affordance

The object and place the robot will actually meet: appliance types, heights, button layouts. If the target scene cannot support the source affordance, the line is flagged, not localized around.

08 / FAILURE

When a check fails

The line is held. It goes to a named human reviewer with the failed check and the evidence; the decision is logged. Unresolved lines ship as open questions.

Paraphrase sets & negative controls

One literal translation is not a localization. A 2026 Greek VLA study found that policies overfit the translator's phrasing, and that training on seven phrasings per task roughly halved the penalty. A separate Greek LIBERO policy card still scored 21.4% on a wrong-instruction control — a reminder that a localized policy can look better than it is. So the deliverable can include a reviewed paraphrase set per task (each phrasing provenance-linked), plus wrong-instruction controls, and we report both rather than a single headline number. Sources →

Checks are ordered: a line that fails referent identity is not evaluated for register. The protocol is versioned, and the verifier report carries the version so two deliveries can be compared.

05 The ledger A worked sheet

Every line leaves a trace.

The ledger adapts the preprint's episode record (Definition 3.4) with a language decision attached: small, typed and boring, so that a dispute a year later is a lookup instead of an argument.

06 Boundaries What changes hands

Scoped on both sides, in writing.

The work is data in, evidence out. Everything else stays on your side of the line, and we write that down before the scope is signed — not after the first disagreement.

  • Target languages, scripts and regional variants
  • Segment annotations and the field map
  • A termbase, or permission to build one

07 Questions Asked before the scope

Fair questions, plain answers.

Short answers here; the long versions are in the method, in the reference list and in the delivery itself. If a question is missing, email it — the answer belongs in intake, not in a brochure.

Is this machine translation with a human check?

No, and the distinction is the product. A model may produce a first pass, but no line is delivered because a model said so: it is delivered because an independent verifier accepted it against the scene, the segment map and the schema. Unverified machine translation is not passed off as human work here — that is one of the things on the do-not list.

Do you need our model weights or access to the robot?

No. We need the dataset export, one sample we can read, the field map, segment annotations, the target languages and an evaluation slice. Nothing is trained on your data, and the ledger records what was read.

What happens when the data cannot settle a referent?

It becomes an open question in the delivery, with the evidence we have and the decision we recommend. We do not resolve it by choosing a plausible noun and hoping the reviewer is in a good mood.

Can you work under NDA?

Yes. Nothing about your data needs to be published: the ledger is yours. Every example on this site is written as an illustrative sample, never as client material — there is no client data on the site at all.

Which languages do you take on?

Founding lines are EN → SR, HR, BS and VI, with DE, ES, FR and JA opening. A line is a named reviewer, a termbase and a verification record — not a language tag in a dropdown. Right-to-left scripts are treated as a layout and alignment problem, not only a text problem.

How is quality reported — and why is there no percentage?

As a record, not a score: pass, pass after revision or hold, each with the failed check and the reviewer. There is no single quality percentage on this site because we have not measured one on your data, and a number invented for a landing page is not evidence.

Is the method peer reviewed?

No. It is a preprint, and the site says so wherever the claims appear. The quality bound it gives is conditional on the verifier; the ledger is how you check the condition. The paper, including the negative result it reports, is public.
